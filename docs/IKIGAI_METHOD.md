# CareerQuest Ikigai and Baseline A

## What this implementation uses

The supplied `ikigai_*.py`, `baseline_a.py`, HTML Venn example and ZIP were used as design references. They are not application instructions and their sample `PROFILE` values were not imported. The repository and ZIP contain no real job-postings table. We use only observed public data in the four-circle implementation, and clearly distinguish occupation estimates from vacancy data. O*NET describes US occupations; its skill and interest profiles are exploratory proxies when viewing Pakistan and have not been locally validated.

| Circle | Person evidence | Occupation / market evidence | Current limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Love | 18 original RIASEC activity ratings, plus same-career simulator enjoyment | O*NET 31.0 occupational interest profile | Three items per interest type are an exploratory screener, **not** the validated O*NET Interest Profiler. Stated-interest text is preserved but not numerically inferred. |
| Good at | Existing Learning DNA answers, plus same-career simulator confidence | O*NET 31.0 ability, skill, knowledge, and work-style importance | Learning DNA is self-report, not a verified skill test; the questionnaire-to-O*NET crosswalk is our hypothesis. Grades/projects are notes until a rubric and validation data exist. |
| World needs | None | US: BLS annual openings and projected 2025–35 employment growth. Pakistan: ILOSTAT ISCO-08 submajor-group employment level in 2025 and change since 2021. | BLS openings are projected opportunities, not counted postings. ILOSTAT employment is neither vacancies nor posting volume. Pakistan SOC-to-ISCO matching uses broad, disclosed proxies. An existing editorial `impact` tag is shown separately but not scored; social impact needs an outcome dataset. |
| Paid for | Country selection | US: BLS 2025 occupation median annual USD. Pakistan: ILOSTAT 2025 median hourly PKR for ISCO-08 **major** group. | Broad Pakistani groups give many careers identical pay evidence. Do not compare annual USD with hourly PKR. Numbeo index is country context only, not a salary conversion or wage percentile. |

Data joins are in `scripts/build_ikigai_data.py`; the checked-in snapshot is `frontend/lib/data/occupation-evidence.json`. The original raw downloads are in `data/onet-31/`, `data/ilo-pak-isco2.csv`, and `data/ilo-pak-earnings.csv`. Rebuild from the repository root with `python scripts/build_ikigai_data.py`. The crosswalk covers all 13 Learning DNA dimensions. O*NET 31.0 uses Essential and Transferable Skills, and its Work Styles Impact (`WI`) range is -3 to +3 rather than the 1–5 Importance (`IM`) scale; the builder normalizes them separately using the [official scale reference](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/31.0/csv/scales_reference.html). The intrapersonal and adaptability links to work styles are especially tentative modelling choices.

## Scoring and Venn

Each RIASEC type is the mean of its three ratings rescaled from 1–5 to 0–1. The interest score for a career is the O*NET interest-importance-weighted mean of answered types. A completed 18-item profile is recommended; the UI marks missing types as unavailable. The Learning DNA score uses only dimensions with answered prompts; each dimension is shrunk toward 0.5 with three pseudo-observations, then weighted by O*NET importance. Same-career enjoyment or confidence contributes 20% when a quiz score exists, or supplies the only personal signal if the quiz has no answers. These weights and shrinkage are explicit modelling choices and require validation; they are **not** coefficients from a research paper.

US needs is the mean of the growth and annual-openings percentile among the eight displayed occupations. Pakistan needs is the mean of submajor employment-change and employment-level percentiles among those eight proxies. US paid is a median-wage percentile among those eight occupations. Pakistan paid is a median-earnings percentile among **all nine published ISCO-08 major groups**, so all eight displayed careers share the same broad-group proxy and cannot be distinguished on pay. Ties receive average rank. US market percentiles are **within the app's eight occupations**, not percentiles over the whole labor market. Missing evidence is `null`, not 0 or 50. The four-circle summary is a geometric mean and is unavailable until all four circles have data. It should be interpreted only as an exploratory ordering.

The diagram uses four fixed, overlapping rotated ellipses. The code samples the plane and places a career dot only at a point whose geometric set membership exactly equals the career's thresholded circle membership. This is a set-membership visualization; dot distance and area do not encode score or probability. Careers below all thresholds remain in the accompanying list.

Cost adjustment does not change a **within-country** pay percentile: dividing every wage in the same country by that country's single Numbeo index preserves the order exactly. The index also has no wage units, so showing a “cost-adjusted USD/PKR salary” from that division would be misleading. For valid cross-country purchasing-power pay comparisons, add a consistent wage period, country-specific price/PPP conversion and local tax assumptions; those are not in the current inputs. The app therefore shows the Numbeo index as context and keeps country wage ranks separate.

## Baseline A: develop and evaluate it step by step

1. **Define the candidate set.** Start with the eight O*NET occupation titles and descriptions already in the checked-in snapshot. For a real market baseline, supply a licensed or otherwise permitted CSV of actual postings with `title,description,country`, and optionally `occupation_id,skills`. Verify collection dates, country, duplicates and coverage. No sample postings are silently invented.
2. **Create occupation documents.** Run `python backend/baseline_a.py --profile "Python statistics and research"`. With `--jobs data/job_postings.csv --country PK`, the loader groups actual posting text by occupation ID or normalized title and country. Inspect the title mapping manually, especially data analyst/data scientist and product manager/project management specialist proxies.
3. **Build the user document.** Join only user-provided skills and interests (and verified projects if available) into a short string. Keep salaries, BLS demand, ILOSTAT statistics, Ikigai ratings and simulator outcome scores out of Baseline A. This preserves an independent comparison.
4. **Fit TF-IDF on occupation text only.** `backend/baseline_a.py` tokenizes text, uses log-scaled term frequency, smoothed inverse document frequency, and L2 normalization. The query uses the same vocabulary and IDF. The dot product of L2-normalized vectors is cosine similarity. Review common words and title/skill field weighting on held-out examples, not on test labels. If the corpus becomes large, swap in scikit-learn `TfidfVectorizer` with equivalent settings.
5. **Rank and inspect.** The output contains occupation ID, title, cosine value, shared terms, and actual posting count only when a postings file is supplied. Filter by country before fitting if evaluating a country-specific corpus. A zero-overlap interactive query raises an error so it cannot look like a valid match; during evaluation it counts as a miss.
6. **Create independent relevance labels.** Recruit users or trained reviewers to label which occupation IDs are acceptable for a profile. Do not derive labels from this model or from Ikigai scores. Split users by identity into development and held-out test sets; if posting dates are available, also do a temporal holdout. Do not let the same person's paraphrases cross splits.
7. **Measure the baseline.** Store held-out cases as `[ {"profile":"...", "relevant_ids":["data"]} ]` and run `python backend/baseline_a.py --labels heldout.json -k 5`. The script reports Recall@5 and mean reciprocal rank. Report number of users, country, label agreement, missing-label policy and confidence intervals; inspect performance by subgroup and by occupation. Compare the same users and candidate set against Ikigai with user-level paired bootstrap intervals, then run an opt-in user study before deployment.
8. **Improve only after evaluation.** Tune text cleaning, synonyms, and occupation mapping on the development split. Add embeddings as a second baseline if TF-IDF misses semantic matches. Never claim that a higher offline score proves better career outcomes.

The application keeps its original exploratory career ranking separate from this Python text baseline and from the new Ikigai view. Neither changes the others' data or model weights.

## Sources and research

- [O*NET 31.0 database](https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html): occupation descriptions, interests, abilities, skills, knowledge and work styles. O*NET SOC occupation titles are proxies for some app career names.
- [O*NET Interest Profiler research and validation](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/IP_RVS.html) and [technical manual](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IP_Manual.pdf): support the RIASEC construct and **validated O*NET instruments**, not this original 18-item set. For a measured interest scale, replace this screener with the public O*NET 30-item Mini-IP and its prescribed scoring.
- [BLS occupational projections table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-separations-and-openings.htm) and [BLS wage tables](https://www.bls.gov/oes/tables.htm): US openings, growth and median annual wages. The data snapshot also links occupation-specific BLS OOH pages.
- [ILOSTAT data catalog](https://ilostat.ilo.org/data/?cat_mode=subject), [Pakistan employment series](https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator?id=EMP_TEMP_SEX_OC2_NB&ref_area=PAK), [Pakistan earnings series](https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator?id=EAR_EHRM_SEX_OCU_CUR_NB&ref_area=PAK), and [ISCO-08 classification](https://isco.ilo.org/en/isco-08/): country-specific employment and earnings at their published aggregation level.
- [Numbeo 2026 Mid-Year Cost of Living Plus Rent Index](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/rankings_by_country.jsp?displayColumn=2): user-contributed cost context; not a wage statistic or official PPP adjustment.
- [Salton, Wong and Yang, 1975, “A Vector Space Model for Automatic Indexing”](https://doi.org/10.1145/361219.361220): vector-space retrieval foundation for the text baseline. [scikit-learn TF-IDF documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/feature_extraction.html#tfidf-term-weighting) explains the common smoothed-IDF variant implemented here.

No paper validates the current four-circle weighting, threshold, crosswalk, or predicts individual career satisfaction. Those choices are disclosed for testing rather than presented as empirical findings.
